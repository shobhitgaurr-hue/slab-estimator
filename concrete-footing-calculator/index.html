Understanding Structural Concrete Footing Sizing & Depth

A concrete footing serves as the foundation for structural loads, including decks, pergolas, retaining walls, and home extensions. The main goal of a footing is to distribute the highly concentrated vertical weight of a post or wall across a broader surface area of soil. Because footings carry heavy vertical structural weights, they must be designed differently than surface slabs.

Typical Footing Size Ranges

Footing sizes depend on the weight load of the structure and local soil load capacity:

  • Standard Deck Post Footings (12" x 12" or 16" x 16"): Typically square or circular piers, poured 12" to 36" deep. Commonly utilizes cardboard sonotube forms.
  • Large Structural Footings (24" x 24"): Used for heavy structures, tall decks, or areas with low soil bearing capacity.
  • Wall Trench Footings (18" to 24" Wide): Continuous concrete runs that support masonry, retaining walls, or foundation walls.

Pro Tips for Structural Footing pours

  1. Always Dig Below the Frost Line: Footings must extend below your region's maximum frost penetration depth. If water in the soil under a footing freezes, the resulting frost heave can lift the footing, damaging the structural framing above.
  2. Clean Out Loose Soil: Before pouring concrete, remove all loose dirt, roots, and water from the bottom of the footing hole. Loose dirt compresses under load, causing structural settling. Pour concrete directly onto solid, undisturbed subgrade.
  3. Anchor Structural Connectors: Do not just stick wooden posts directly in concrete. Install metal anchor brackets (such as post bases) into the wet concrete. This prevents wood rot and ensures a secure structural attachment.

Footing Concrete FAQs

❓ How deep should a concrete footing be?

Concrete footings must be poured below your local area's frost line (typically 12 to 48 inches deep depending on region) to prevent ground frost heaving from lifting and damaging the structure.

❓ What strength concrete is needed for structural footings?

Structural building codes generally require a minimum of 3,000 PSI concrete for load-bearing footings. High-early strength mixes can be used to accelerate frame construction schedules.

❓ Do footings need rebar reinforcement?

Yes, load-bearing footings require rebar reinforcement (usually horizontal steel grids or vertical rebar pins connected to posts) to distribute vertical loads and prevent structural shear failures.
