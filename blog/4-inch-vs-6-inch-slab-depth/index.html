4-Inch vs 6-Inch Concrete Slab Depth: Comparison Guide

When preparing to pour concrete for a residential or light commercial project, one of the most critical decisions is determining the correct slab thickness. The thickness of a concrete slab directly dictates its load capacity, overall lifespan, structural reinforcement requirements, and project costs.

In the building industry, the two most common depths for standard concrete slabs are 4 inches (10 cm) and 6 inches (15 cm). Choosing the wrong depth can lead to premature cracking under heavy loads, or unnecessary material costs.

Quick Comparison

Typical Use Cases Patios, sidewalks, walkways, hot tub pads, small garden sheds. Driveways, garage floors, heavy equipment pads, commercial walkways.

When to Use 4 Inches

A 4-inch concrete slab is the standard minimum for residential concrete slabs. It is suitable for applications where the only traffic will be foot traffic or static light loads. If you are constructing a standard garden walkway, a patio for lawn furniture, or a pad for a small wooden shed, 4 inches is highly cost-effective and structurally sufficient, provided the subgrade is compacted and stable.

When to Use 6 Inches

A 6-inch concrete slab is required for driveways, parking pads, garage floors, and storage areas for heavy machinery. Passenger vehicles, SUV, and delivery trucks exert significant localized pressure on concrete. A 4-inch slab under a heavy vehicle can crack or subside over time, especially at corners or joints. Increasing the slab thickness to 6 inches increases its load-bearing capacity by approximately 2.25 times compared to a 4-inch slab.

Cost Implications

Because concrete is sold by volume (cubic yards), going from a 4-inch depth to a 6-inch depth increases the concrete volume requirement by exactly **50%**. For a 20' x 20' driveway (400 sq. ft.):

  • At 4" depth: requires approx. 5.4 cubic yards (with buffer).
  • At 6" depth: requires approx. 8.1 cubic yards (with buffer).

This results in an extra 2.7 cubic yards of concrete, which can add significant cost depending on local concrete delivery rates. However, investing in the extra depth upfront prevents expensive slab replacement costs later.
